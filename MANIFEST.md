# Informed Opinion

## Purpose

In many cases people's opinions are based on scant knowledge of the
question they are opining on, and can thus be faulty.

The purpose of this site is to gather people's opinions on a wide
range of subjects, and weight each opinion based on how much knowledge
of the subject the opiner has been able to demonstrate.

In this way, the "informed opinion" on various questions can be determined.

The ideal goal of this project would be to provide a resource that can
be consulted to find the opinions of well-informed persons on a wide
range of topics, as well as a kind of forum where people can register
their opinions and demonstrate their knowledge of the relevant areas.

So visitors to the site are invited to answer "opinion" questions, such as

> Which of the following best reflects your view?

> Human activities (such as burning fossil fuels and deforestation)
> are the main cause of the global warming that has occurred over the
> past several decades.

> - Definitely true
> - Probably true
> - Neutral
> - Probably false
> - Definitely false

The user is informed that their opinion has a weight of zero, since
they have not yet demonstrated any relevant knowledge.

They then take a quiz consisting of a series of "fact" questions on
the subject of climate change: questions with factual (and easily
referenced) answers that would be likely to influence a person's
opinion on the above.

Since at the outset we know the opinion of the user, and we know of
each fact question which way its answer is likely to influence
opinion, the order of the quiz questions can be arranged so as to
minimise possible resistance to evidence.

Quiz questions appear one at a time. Answers are multiple choice. Once
the user has made a choice, she is informed
- whether it is the correct response
- what the correct answer is, and why, and links to the evidence
- how the response has affected the weight of the user's opinion on the subject

Then the next quiz question appears.

The user may stop the quiz at any time so as to move to a different
opinion question, or to retake the quiz on the same opinion question
all over again. However, the quiz questions will not appear in the
same order as before: already-answered questions will tend to have
lower precedence than unanswered questions, and the order will be more
random with every retake. This will make it more difficult to "game"
the system.

The weight of a user's opinion on a given opinion question is the
proportion of the available fact-question importance that the user has
earned by answering correctly, counting only the most recent answer to
each fact question. Each fact has a published importance of Supporting
(1), Significant (2), or Foundational (3), together with a rationale.
The range is deliberately narrow so that no single editorial judgement
can dominate a quiz. The unweighted proportion of correct answers is
also retained and displayed for auditability. Until a question bank has
undergone an explicit importance review, all its facts remain at the
standard Supporting weight.

The collective informed result assigns the five opinion positions signed
values of +1, +0.5, 0, -0.5 and -1. Each user's signed position is multiplied
by that user's topic-specific knowledge weight. The sum is divided by the
total knowledge weight contributed, including knowledgeable neutral opinions,
to produce a bounded aggregate from -1 (NO) through 0 (NEUTRAL) to +1 (YES).
Opinions with zero knowledge weight do not move the result.

In principle, the weight of an opinion only applies to that specific
opinion question, and expertise in one area does not transfer to or
affect any other area. However, it is possible that different opinion
questions might be similar enough that the same fact question might be
relevant in both opinions. In that case, the fact question might
appear in both quizzes, and when answered in one quiz would be
considered answered in the other.
