# Broader editorial review of the Gaza genocide bank

Reviewed on 17 October 2026 using read-only moderator API requests. All fifty
current fingerprints match the applied snapshot. The topic is unpublished.
No question, rating, calibration assessment or publication state was changed.

## Assessment

The bank needs another editorial revision before publication. Its legal
framing and qualifications are useful, but it gives too much knowledge credit
to recall of reports and too little space to a coherent factual baseline.
Some answer choices permit success without knowing the fact. The earlier
mechanical audit was too narrow: it checked longest-answer guessing and did
not check the inverse pattern or semantic clues.

The site's purpose is to weight opinions by demonstrated relevant knowledge.
An individually sourced statement can still be a poor measure of that
knowledge. The important test is whether knowing the answer supplies a
materially different reason to assess the proposition, and whether the item
requires that knowledge to answer correctly.

## Standards and comparison

I read the current API editorial standard, the local editorial standard,
MANIFEST.md and the knowledge/answerability guidance. I examined every prompt
in six published comparator banks and inspected their choices and ratings,
with particular attention to Veganism, Nature of ME/CFS, Gaza war and Has
America gained power. Comparators illustrate practices rather than establish
compliance: several older sampled banks themselves contain questions rated
unfit, and some show answer-length cues.

Useful practices include establishing basic definitions and scale before
technical evidence; testing mechanisms as well as findings; mixing accessible
knowledge with deeper distinctions; including concrete counterevidence; and
asking what the evidence can and cannot establish. Attribution remains
necessary when the fact is an assessment, allegation or contested finding.
The problem is dependence on report recall across so much of a bank, not the
presence of an organisation's name in a prompt.

case names or reporting dates when an accessible, consequential distinction
would do. The power bank shows that this bank's length is not unprecedented;
length alone is not the defect.

## Priority findings

### 1. Answer cues undermine the knowledge score

Choosing the shortest option by character count succeeds on 30 of 50 items.
All thirty correct choices are uniquely shortest; choosing the first shortest
option also scores thirty. It earns 59 of 95 importance points (62.1%).
This is not a measured reader result, but it is a reproducible scoring exploit.
The explicit standard names a longest-answer ceiling; its broader ban on
answer-length cues applies equally to this inverse pattern. Word-count
variants also perform above chance. Matching all choices to an identical
length would be another mechanical distortion: plausible parallel choices
and checks of multiple simple strategies are needed.

There are also semantic cues independent of length:

- C04 (835) asks what contradicts vehicles approaching without emergency
  signals. Only the lights-operating choice supplies that contradiction.
- M05 (814) calls its subject the Gaza Mortality Survey and asks what new
  evidence it used. The field-survey choice can be selected from the title.
- H02 (843) places a genocide in relation to the government's war against a
  rebel movement, then rewards saying the two occurred in the same period.
- C02 (808) and R04 (828) oppose qualified accounts of relief against much
  more absolute accounts of no food, no access or complete recovery.

The first three should provisionally be treated as unfit pending substantive
rewriting, rather than merely assigned a more demanding rating. This review
does not change their stored ratings. Many other items need stronger
substantively plausible distractors. Recalibrate after rewriting.

### 2. Report recall crowds out underlying knowledge

A transparent prompt-form screen identifies 36 of 50 questions beginning
with variants such as what did, how did, or what assessment. That is a review
flag, not a count of defective questions. Several appropriately test what an
authority found. But D06 (829) and D07 (840) chiefly ask readers to identify
an organisation's interpretation of genocidal intent. Memorizing those
interpretations is weaker evidence of understanding than knowing the actual
policies, warnings, alternatives and subsequent decisions.

Reframe source-mediated items around the material observation when that is
justified. Keep attribution in the prompt where necessary; otherwise put it
in the source and explanation. Preserve the difference between the observed
fact and the source's inference. Knowing a finding matters, but familiarity
with a particular reading list should not dominate the weight of an opinion.

### 3. The basic factual picture is incomplete

There is considerable mortality methodology but no clear, period-aligned
baseline for Gaza's population, recorded deaths by the end of the chosen
period, displacement or overall destruction. Hospital function provides one
valuable scale measure. Readers should also be able to assess broader
magnitude without reconstructing it from isolated incidents and partial-year
estimates. Deaths and damage are not substitutes for intent, but remain
material evidence.

The bank mentions military aims and hostages but does not establish the
basic scale of the initiating attacks or hostage-taking. Continued military
capability appears chiefly through one rocket attack, a tunnel visit and an
intelligence assessment. Documented capability and operations across the
period, together with constraints and changes in conduct, would better test
the proposed military explanation. These are candidate gaps requiring source
work, not permission to adopt military claims uncritically.

Do not simply import another bank's totals: align dates, geography, recorded
versus estimated deaths and civilian/combatant uncertainty. Do not infer
combatant status from age or sex, or infer legality from a numerical ratio.
Adding context gives no legal permission for separate violations.

### 4. Redundancy affects the weight assigned to knowledge

Eleven legal items account for 26 of 95 importance points. Six mortality
items account for another ten points. These are not inherently excessive,
but several closely related distinctions earn separate credit: mortality
source, independent verification, representativeness, record modelling,
household surveys and projections. Keep the genuinely distinct concepts;
combine repeated versions when a single well-designed question can test the
same knowledge. Policy announcement and delivery outcome also need separate
items only when each contributes a distinct decision-relevant test.

An obscure fact does not become foundational just because it concerns intent.
Reassess importance after selection, so attention to one cluster does not
silently set how informed a respondent is deemed to be.

### 5. Reading burden and self-containment need attention

Repeated procedural terms, case names and defensive caveats increase the work
needed to identify the central point. D05 (815) asks about a qualification to
Herzog's collective-responsibility statement without supplying that statement.
The issue should be readable in isolation, including the original language,
qualification, role and date needed for the intended distinction.

Explanations should state the finding, why it matters and its decisive limit.
Avoid repeatedly appending a generic reminder that an incident does not prove
genocide. Retain specific limits that could change the conclusion. The
production glossary was unavailable during implementation; the bank uses
supported initialisms and spells out additional names. Tooltip coverage does
not by itself make specialised prose accessible.

### 6. Calibration should follow editorial repair

Forty-two of fifty questions have answerability 3, and none have specialist
knowledge 1. Neither distribution alone violates a rule. Together with the
clear semantic cues and the generic appended calibration rationales, they
warrant fresh item-specific assessment. Easier questions can still test
important knowledge; adding obscure facts to manufacture a difficulty range
would make the problem worse. Human supervisory review is still pending.

### 7. Keep the final inference outside the answer key

Retain the acts-plus-intent distinction, only-reasonable-inference standard,
coexisting purposes, substantial-part requirement and distinction between
interim decisions and final findings. Keep adjudicated historical examples
that demonstrate different classifications. Avoid implying that an acquittal
proves every event was non-genocidal or that a historical analogy classifies
Gaza.

The opinion is a claim about legal classification. A synthesis item should
identify which factual and legal inference remains to be assessed; it should
not call genocide a matter of personal values. The generic standard's
values-synthesis instruction needs that adaptation for this empirical/legal
proposition. There must be room to know the evidence and disagree about the
ultimate inference without losing quiz credit for that disagreement.

## Recommended revision

First repair the obvious answer leaks and the shortest-choice pattern. Then
review every item for a distinct contribution to judging the proposition.
Consolidate overlapping methods and source-interpretation recall to make room
for a dated factual baseline and better operational context. Keep the strong
legal core while putting case names and source detail in explanations where
possible. Rebuild parallel, plausible choices; reassess importance and both
difficulty measures; repeat bank-level and supervisory review.

Do not increase the bank beyond fifty to accommodate these repairs. A
stronger bank may fit in roughly forty to fifty questions after consolidation;
the final number should follow coverage and item quality. There is no reason
to impose equal numbers for and against the proposition.

The assessment below concerns the current item, not the worth of its subject.
“Keep core” still permits prose and answer-cue improvements. Merge candidates
must preserve materially different knowledge rather than bundle several
independent questions into one.

claim, an independent expert legal opinion, or a participant study. Current
API reads established the content reviewed and publication state. No site's
editorial records were changed. Source research would be needed for proposed
new factual coverage before it could be saved.
