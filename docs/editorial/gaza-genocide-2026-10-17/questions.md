# Revised Gaza genocide fact bank

The topic remains unpublished. Correct answers below are editorial keys,
not answers submitted by participants.

## L01

Under the Genocide Convention, what must be established for conduct to
constitute genocide?

A. A listed prohibited act committed knowing that severe civilian suffering is
   likely to result
B. A listed prohibited act committed with intent to destroy a protected group,
   in whole or in part
C. A campaign of exceptionally destructive violence that kills a large share
   of the civilian population
D. A declared intention to destroy a protected group, whether or not a
   prohibited act is committed

Correct: B

Article II requires acts and specific group-destructive intent together.
Neither intention without a prohibited act nor foreseeable civilian suffering
alone establishes completed genocide. The same physical outcome can require
different legal classification if the proven intent differs.

Source: [United Nations — Genocide Convention, Article II](https://www.un.org/en/genocide-prevention/1948-convention)

## P01

What did Defence Minister Yoav Gallant announce for Gaza on 9 October 2023, as
recorded by the ICJ?

A. A weapons embargo while civilian food and fuel deliveries continued
   normally
B. A temporary closure of one crossing while all other supply routes stayed
   open
C. A restriction on military fuel depots while electricity and civilian
   supplies were exempted
D. A complete siege cutting electricity, food and fuel supplies

Correct: D

The announcement concerned necessities for the population, not only individual
military targets. It is evidence of a stated policy by a senior decision-
maker. Its implementation, duration, subsequent changes and purpose still
require examination; the announcement alone is not a genocide finding.

Source: [International Court of Justice — Order of 26 January 2024, recorded official statements](https://www.icj-cij.org/sites/default/files/case-related/192/192-20240126-ord-01-00-en.pdf)

## M01

## L02

Which method of destruction is expressly included in Article II of the
Genocide Convention?

A. Preventing the group from exercising political autonomy while preserving
   its physical survival
B. Deliberately imposing living conditions calculated to bring about the
   group’s physical destruction
C. Destroying cultural institutions with the purpose of assimilating the group
   into a different culture
D. Compelling the group to relocate while intending its continued physical
   survival in another place

Correct: B

Starvation and deprivation can fall within Article II(c) when its conditions
and the additional specific intent are established. Genocide need not take the
form of direct executions. Political suppression, cultural destruction and
displacement require separate legal analysis.

Source: [United Nations — Genocide Convention, Article II](https://www.un.org/en/genocide-prevention/1948-convention)

## R01

What did WHO report about the second round of Gaza’s polio campaign completed
in November 2024?

A. Over 550,000 children received a second dose under local pauses, while some
   northern areas remained inaccessible
B. About 550,000 children received a second dose, with full access to the
   northern areas targeted in the first round
C. The second round reached central and southern Gaza, but its northern phase
   was cancelled without any vaccinations
D. The campaign achieved at least 90% coverage in every community, with no

Correct: A

WHO reported 556,774 second doses and estimated that 7,000–10,000 children in

universal protection nor a token measure without effect.

## L04

When genocidal intent is inferred from a pattern of conduct, what merits test
did the ICJ apply in Croatia v. Serbia?

A. It must be the only reasonable inference from the pattern
B. It must be more likely than any single competing inference
C. It must be one credible inference supported by the pattern
D. It must be the inference supported by the largest number of incidents

Correct: A

The test is stricter than choosing the most persuasive explanation. A
reasonable account without genocidal intent matters; an imagined possibility
unsupported by evidence is not automatically reasonable. Failure to establish
this inference does not itself prove that genocidal intent was absent.

Source: [International Court of Justice — Summary of judgment, 3 February 2015](https://www.icj-cij.org/node/103932)

## L08

Under the ICRC’s explanation of targeting law, how is anticipated civilian
harm assessed in an attack on a military objective?

A. Against the civilian losses suffered by the attacking state during the
   original enemy attack
B. Against the concrete and direct military advantage anticipated, alongside
   precautionary duties
C. Against the total strategic benefit of defeating the enemy, regardless of
   the particular attack
D. Against the eventual number of enemy fighters killed, using the outcome
   rather than prior expectations

Correct: B

Civilian deaths do not alone establish an unlawful attack; a military target
does not alone establish a lawful one. Proportionality concerns expected harm
and anticipated advantage, with feasible precautions. That targeting
assessment is distinct from establishing genocidal intent.

Source: [ICRC — Targeting under international humanitarian law](https://casebook.icrc.org/highlight/targeting-under-international-humanitarian-law)

## L03

According to the ICJ’s genocide case law, how does forced displacement relate
to destruction of a protected group?

A. Permanent expulsion is itself the required destruction when the displaced
   population cannot return home
B. Expulsion constitutes destruction when it removes a majority of the group
   from a particular territory
C. Expulsion and physical or biological destruction are distinct, although
   displacement can contribute to genocide
D. Expulsion constitutes destruction when authorities intend to erase the
   group’s historical connection to its land

Correct: C

The ICJ distinguishes physical or biological destruction from making an area
ethnically homogeneous through removal. Displacement may still be a grave
crime and evidence of genocide when the relevant acts and destructive intent
are established.

Source: [International Court of Justice — Croatia v. Serbia, 2015 judgment](https://www.icj-cij.org/node/103223)

## H03

Why did the ICJ reject Croatia’s genocide claim against Serbia in 2015 despite
finding killings and serious harm?

A. The killings were too few to reach the Convention’s minimum numerical
   threshold
B. The killings were excluded because they occurred during a recognized armed
   conflict
C. The victims were not members of a national or ethnic group protected by the
   Convention
D. The required specific intent to destroy the Croat group had not been proved

Correct: D

The Court distinguished prohibited physical acts from the additional

## L06

What distinction did the Jelisić Appeals Chamber draw between a perpetrator’s
motive and genocidal intent?

A. A personal motive can coexist with the specific intent to destroy a
   protected group
B. A political motive replaces specific intent when destroying a group serves
   a wider objective
C. A financial motive excludes specific intent when the perpetrator
   principally seeks material gain
D. A personal motive establishes specific intent when the perpetrator targets
   people of one ethnicity

Correct: A

The judgment distinguishes why a perpetrator seeks an outcome from whether
group destruction is intended. A further purpose is not automatic disproof of
destructive intent, and is not proof of it either. Whether military aims
coexist with such intent in Gaza remains an evidential question.

Source: [International tribunal — Jelisić appeal judgment, 5 July 2001](https://www.icty.org/x/cases/jelisic/acjug/en/jel-aj010705e.htm)

## M05

What new evidence did the Gaza Mortality Survey, published in February 2026,

A. A census of every Gaza household, yielding exactly 75,200 identified
   civilian deaths

## L07

Under the international rules of state responsibility, how is conduct by
military units acting as state organs generally treated?

A. As conduct attributable to the state, including when official action
   exceeds their authority
B. As conduct attributable to the state only after individual soldiers have
   been criminally convicted
C. As private conduct unless the head of government personally approved the
   particular operation

Correct: A

## L09

## L05

In the Jelisić appeal judgment, did proving genocidal intent require proof of
an explicit plan or policy?

A. Yes; an explicit plan was required when the accused acted as part of an
   organized force
B. Yes; circumstantial evidence could establish participation but not the
   intent to destroy a group
C. No; a plan can be evidence, but intent may also be inferred from the
   circumstances

   was presumed

Correct: C

The Appeals Chamber said a plan or policy is not a legal ingredient, although

## R04

How did the IPC’s December 2025 assessment describe conditions following the
