# Gaza genocide bank: implementation review

The proposition covers conduct between 7 October 2023 and 31 December 2025.
It asks whether genocide occurred within that period, rather than asserting
that every operation had the same purpose. Later research can inform facts
about that period. The ultimate genocide conclusion is never a scored key.

The review considered 70 candidates across 12 evidence sequences. The
40-item comparison records the specific omissions caused by the shorter
version. Fifty questions retain material distinctions about verification,
relief, operational evidence, reproductive harm and continuing armed
resistance. This is a coverage judgment, not a claim that fifty questions
can settle the dispute or reproduce a trial.

The central comparison is between military defeat of Hamas with extreme
civilian disregard and prohibited acts committed with group-destructive
intent, possibly alongside military purposes. The former is not assumed
lawful; the latter is not established by the severity of harm alone.
The legal questions distinguish motive from intent, expulsion from physical
or biological destruction, attribution from individual conviction, and
interim measures from merits findings. Acts and intent are both necessary.

The strongest challenge to a genocide inference includes actual armed
resistance, military infrastructure, stated military aims, effective relief,
policy changes, alternative explanations and limits of selected incidents.
The strongest challenge to a non-genocidal explanation includes specific
population-wide policies, deliberate destruction of survival infrastructure,
renewed restrictions, warnings, actual access outcomes and the relationship
between official statements and implementation. Neither account is presumed
to explain every episode merely because it can explain aggregate damage.

Evidence direction follows the selected facts: 17 supporting, 10 countering
and 23 contextual or mixed. There is no numerical neutrality target.
Individual items qualify source claims, geographical scope, sampling,
verification, attribution and uncertainty. A tribunal's finding, a military
inquiry and an advocacy organization's inference are identified separately.
The mortality questions distinguish ministry records, independently verified
subsets, overlapping record models, household surveys and projections.
Neither adult male identity nor a headline total establishes combatant status.

Source retrieval and verification limits are recorded in the source ledger.
Some official or publisher pages restricted direct access; indexed primary
text, accessible official documents and corroborating reports were used as
specified. A successful HTTP response alone was not treated as verification:
several publisher responses contained access shells. The OHCHR verification
report was downloaded and its methodological footnote checked directly.
This is an editorial desk review, not independent legal expert review or a
reader calibration study. Ratings remain proposals for supervisory review.

The production glossary endpoint is not yet deployed. Existing supported
initialisms are used; additional organization names are spelled out. No
application deployment is needed for these editorial changes.

The user confirms that the unpublished questions have no participant
answers. Existing forty records therefore receive substantive revisions;
ten additional records are created. The before snapshot and replacement map
record the previous content and intended changes. Server-side revision audits
provide the corresponding write history. Publication is not requested.

Run `python3 script/editorial/audit_gaza_genocide_bank.py` for mechanical
validation. All fifty records also passed Rails model validation against an
isolated local test database without saving records. The machine audit does
not establish factual accuracy or plausible distractors; those require the
editorial review represented by the research and source records.
