# Revised Gaza genocide questions: broader editorial revision

The topic remains unpublished. Answer keys are editorial keys.

## L01

What distinguishes genocide from other grave crimes against a civilian
population?

A. A campaign of mass expulsion intended to remove a protected group from its
   homeland
B. A prohibited act with intent to destroy a protected group, in whole or in
   part
C. A widespread attack on civilians intended to terrorise them into political
   submission
D. A prohibited act together with knowledge that extensive civilian deaths
   will result from a military operation

Correct: B

Genocide requires a listed act and specific group-destructive intent.
Foreseeable deaths, forced removal and other serious crimes can be relevant
evidence, but do not replace that intent.

Source: [United Nations — Genocide Convention, Article II](https://www.un.org/en/genocide-prevention/1948-convention)

Approximately how many people were killed in Israel in the Hamas-led attacks

A. Around 300 people
B. Around 1,200 people
C. Around 3,000 people
D. Around 700 people

Correct: B

Officially reported figures put the toll at about 1,200, including civilians
and security personnel. This establishes the scale of the initiating attack,
not a legal justification for subsequent violations.

## R01

B. Northern areas were reached, but central Gaza was not
C. Northern areas were reached, but displaced children in Rafah were excluded

## L04

For genocidal intent inferred from a pattern of conduct, what test did the ICJ
apply at the final-decision stage?

A. It must be more probable than each alternative
B. It must be plausible and show a serious risk
C. It must follow from a pattern and an express written policy
D. It must be the only reasonable inference from the pattern

Correct: D

The Croatia v. Serbia judgment requires the only reasonable inference for this
mode of proof. A written plan is not indispensable. A merely imaginable
alternative need not be reasonable; failure of proof is not proof that intent
was absent.

Source: [International Court of Justice — Summary of judgment, 3 February 2015](https://www.icj-cij.org/node/103932)

## L08

When an attack targets a military objective, what does the proportionality
rule compare?

A. Expected civilian harm with the overall strategic benefit of winning the
   war
B. Expected civilian harm with the concrete and direct military advantage
   anticipated
C. Actual civilian deaths with the number of enemy fighters eventually killed
D. Civilian casualties in the attack with casualties suffered in the enemy’s
   initial assault

Correct: B

The assessment concerns the particular attack and what could be anticipated,
alongside feasible precautions. A military target does not settle
proportionality; that inquiry is also distinct from genocidal intent.

Source: [ICRC — Targeting under international humanitarian law](https://casebook.icrc.org/highlight/targeting-under-international-humanitarian-law)

## L02

Besides killing, which conduct is expressly listed as a possible act of
genocide?

A. Expelling the group from its homeland to change the territory’s ethnic
   balance
B. Destroying the group’s political institutions to end its territorial
   autonomy
C. Preventing the group from using its language to impose cultural
   assimilation
D. Inflicting living conditions calculated to bring about the group’s physical
   destruction

Correct: D

Article II includes destructive living conditions. The required specific
intent must also be established; cultural suppression and expulsion are
distinct from physical or biological destruction.

Source: [United Nations — Genocide Convention, Article II](https://www.un.org/en/genocide-prevention/1948-convention)

## L03

What separates ethnic cleansing by expulsion from genocide in ICJ case law?

A. Removal within a state is ethnic cleansing, while cross-border removal is
   genocide
B. Expulsion becomes genocide when most of the local population is displaced
C. Removal is distinct from physical or biological destruction
D. Expulsion becomes genocide when the removal is intended to be permanent

Correct: C

Expulsion may be a grave crime and can contribute to genocide. It is not
automatically the destruction required by the Convention, even when permanent
removal is intended.

Source: [International Court of Justice — Croatia v. Serbia, 2015 judgment](https://www.icj-cij.org/node/103223)

## L06

Can a purpose such as gaining territory coexist with genocidal intent?

A. No, if territorial gain is the principal motive
B. Yes, if civilian deaths are expected, regardless of destructive intent
C. No, when destruction serves a further goal rather than an ultimate one
D. Yes; group destruction can be intended alongside a territorial objective

Correct: D

The Jelisić appeal distinguishes motive from specific intent. A further
purpose can coexist with destructive intent, but the existence of that further
purpose proves neither its presence nor its absence.

Source: [International tribunal — Jelisić appeal judgment, 5 July 2001](https://www.icty.org/x/cases/jelisic/acjug/en/jel-aj010705e.htm)

## M05

What evidence underpinned the 2026 study estimating about 75,200 violent
deaths in Gaza by early January 2025?

A. Statistical matching of hospital, morgue and family-registration lists
B. Interviews with a weighted sample of 2,000 households
C. Satellite damage counts combined with neighbourhood population estimates
D. Indirect-mortality ratios extrapolated from other recent wars

Correct: B

The study surveyed 2,000 households and estimated 75,200 violent deaths, with

Separately, individually verified cases are not automatically a representative

## L07

If a state military unit exceeds its orders while acting in an official
capacity, how is its conduct generally attributed?

A. It is attributed to the state only after a commander is convicted
B. It is attributed to the state only after the political leadership endorses
   it
C. It becomes private conduct when the unit breaches its official instructions
D. It remains attributable to the state under state-responsibility rules

Correct: D

authority. Attribution of an act does not itself establish genocidal intent or
a state policy of group destruction.

Source: [International Law Commission — Articles on State Responsibility](https://legal.un.org/ilc/texts/instruments/english/draft_articles/9_6_2001.pdf)

## S01

remains in assessing genocide?

A. Whether group destruction was also intended in committing prohibited acts
B. Whether the military aim was the principal motive
C. Whether civilian harm outweighed the overall military gains
D. Whether a written destruction order exists, as required before intent can
   be inferred

Correct: A

Acts and specific intent must be established together. A military purpose can
coexist with destructive intent; a reasonable explanation without it matters
to inference. Disregard for civilian life is not automatically equivalent to
an intent to destroy the group.
