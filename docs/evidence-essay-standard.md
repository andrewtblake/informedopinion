# Editorial standard for evidence essays

## Purpose

An evidence essay gives readers a useful route into an opinion question without
requiring them to take its fact quiz. It turns the bank's discrete evidence into
a coherent account while preserving the project's neutrality, source
inspectability and separation of facts from values.

An essay is a separately reviewed editorial work. It is not generated from live
fact-question text on each request and is not a substitute for reviewing the
underlying bank.

## Required qualities

Every published evidence essay must:

1. Address the exact proposition rather than the general topic suggested by its
   title.
2. Establish the principal issues promptly in language accessible to an
   interested non-specialist.
3. Cover every foundational issue and the strongest material evidence and
   counterevidence. It need not reproduce every fact question.
4. Treat opposing evidence with comparable precision and seriousness without
   manufacturing numerical balance or false equivalence.
5. Distinguish observed facts, associations, causal conclusions, modelled
   scenarios and predictions.
6. State material uncertainty, limitations, populations, jurisdictions and dates
   where they affect interpretation.
7. Separate factual consequences from the moral or political judgement that
   remains for the reader.
8. Link claims to the same primary, official or authoritative sources required
   by the fact-question standard. Citations must appear close enough to their
   claims for a reader to inspect the evidential chain.
9. Use its own prose. Question wording, answer choices and explanations are
   inputs to synthesis rather than paragraphs to concatenate.
10. Avoid test language, answer-position references and assumptions about which
    facts the reader already knows.
11. Avoid personalised evidence selection or ordering based on the reader's
    opinion. Every reader receives the same factual body and qualifications.
12. State its last editorial review date and provide a route to report
    inaccuracy, bias, unclear wording or outdated evidence.

## Coverage rather than completeness

Editors maintain a private coverage record mapping active fact questions to the
essay. Each item is marked:

- **direct:** the central fact is stated;
- **synthesised:** its substance is combined with related evidence;
- **omitted:** it is unnecessary to the article, with a concise reason; or
- **unresolved:** it still requires an editorial decision.

There is no minimum percentage of individual questions that must be included.
The coverage review instead asks:

- Is every foundational issue represented?
- Is any strong good-faith argument or counterargument missing?
- Are important qualifications being sacrificed for brevity?
- Does an omitted fact expose a materially different consequence, mechanism or
  uncertainty?
- Does the essay remain useful and readable after all necessary evidence is
  included?

Supporting questions, duplicated illustrations and fine technical details may
be omitted. A foundational fact should normally be direct or synthesised; an
omission requires a specific rationale and whole-essay review.

## Structure and tone

An essay should normally:

1. introduce the exact question and its main factual dimensions;
2. organise evidence by issue or causal relationship rather than by quiz order;
3. put apparently conflicting findings together and explain why they can
   coexist;
4. introduce meaningful limitations early rather than saving all qualifications
   for the end; and
5. close with what the evidence establishes, what remains uncertain and what
   evidence cannot decide.

The canonical body should be clear enough for both Informed Opinion and What's
Your View?. Presentation-specific titles, introductions, summaries, navigation
and calls to action may use each site's established voice. They must not alter
the substantive claims, emphasis, qualifications, sources or conclusion.

Two full prose versions should be created only after reader evidence shows that
presentation framing is insufficient. If introduced, both versions must share a
claim-and-source manifest, revision state and simultaneous editorial review.

## Relationship to the quiz

The essay may reveal answers to the fact quiz. This is acceptable because a
score records knowledge demonstrated at the time of answering, not unaided prior
knowledge. The interface must not describe the quiz as a closed-book examination
or the score as proof of pre-existing expertise.

The essay should invite readers to take the quiz, register or revise an opinion,
and inspect the complete fact bank. Taking the quiz must not be required to read
the essay or its sources.

## Provenance and revisions

Each substantive essay claim should be linked editorially to one or more fact
questions and sources. The published prose need not expose internal fact IDs.

A changed or withdrawn linked fact marks the essay for review; it must not
silently rewrite the published article. Review then determines whether:

- no prose change is needed;
- a cosmetic essay correction is sufficient;
- a new essay revision is required; or
- the essay must be temporarily withdrawn because a foundational passage is no
  longer supported.

Essay revisions do not recalculate quiz responses or user scores. Changes to the
underlying fact questions continue to follow the fact-question response policy.

Before publication, an editor checks that every source remains accessible and
supports the relevant claim, the coverage record has no unresolved items, the
two site presentations preserve the same evidence, and the essay is no longer
than its subject requires.
