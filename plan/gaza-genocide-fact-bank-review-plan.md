# Gaza genocide fact-bank review plan

Status: proposed editorial plan, extended on 17 October 2026. The live API was
read for topic 26 and its 40 facts. The topic remains unpublished. This plan
does not authorize applying replacements or publishing the topic.

## Objective and legal framing

Give readers the evidence needed to assess prohibited acts, specific intent,
and attribution to the state. Keep the final disputed inference in the opinion
response; do not turn agreement with either interpretation into a quiz answer.

Use physical or biological destruction of a protected group, in whole or in
substantial part, as the relevant meaning of destruction. Distinguish this
from removing an armed organisation, displacement, cultural destruction, and
reckless or disproportionate harm. Those distinctions do not make other harms
lawful or exclude their evidential relevance to genocide.

The user's proposed focus on explaining the conduct is useful, with two
qualifications. The ICJ's merits test for intent inferred from a pattern is
the only reasonable inference, rather than merely the best explanation.
Military objectives and group-destructive intent must not be assumed mutually
exclusive. Explain what is established law, judicial disagreement, a state's
submission, or an organisation's legal interpretation.

Do not require a genocidal explanation for every attack or the entire war.
Equally, do not infer a state policy from an isolated atrocity or an official's
statement without addressing authority, context and implementation.

Propose a fixed evidence cut-off and an explicit scope before rewriting the
opinion. Distinguish a claim that qualifying conduct occurred during the
period from a claim that every phase or operation was genocidal. State-level
responsibility, individual criminal responsibility and the duty to prevent
genocide are separate questions. Any substantive proposition change requires
review of its effect on existing responses.

Legal anchors:

- [ICJ summary, Croatia v. Serbia, 3 February 2015](https://www.icj-cij.org/node/103932)
- [ICJ summary, Bosnia v. Serbia, 26 February 2007](https://www.icj-cij.org/index.php/node/103898)
- [Ireland's declaration of intervention: a state's interpretation](https://www.icj-cij.org/index.php/node/205093)
- [ICRC explanation of targeting rules](https://casebook.icrc.org/highlight/targeting-under-international-humanitarian-law)

## Initial plan addressing the sceptical critique

1. Retain necessary legal foundations and replace duplicative historical
   lessons with military-context and intent evidence.
2. Establish what is independently documented about armed-group operations,
   civilian infrastructure and specific military objectives. Distinguish
   intelligence assessments, visible evidence and uncorroborated assertions.
3. Explain lawful targeting, proportionality and feasible precautions. Neither
   civilian deaths nor a military target alone determines legality.
4. Examine actual precautions and their results, rather than declared policy
   alone or failures alone. Include evidence of effective relief and changes
   to operations where available.
5. Audit casualty and combatant estimates by the same rules: definitions,
   provenance, methods, uncertainty and corroboration. Do not invent a reliable
   civilian-combatant ratio where the evidence cannot establish one.
6. Test selected quotations through context, translation, authority, orders and
   subsequent conduct. Replace generic questions about what lawyers argued.
7. Distinguish aid authorization, entry, collection, distribution and household
   access. Examine dated explanations for shortages and policy changes.
8. Connect concrete findings to legal elements without treating institutional
   conclusions or dramatic examples as substitutes for that connection.

## Critique from someone persuaded genocide occurred

### The current bank

The strongest criticism is fragmentation. The bank documents many harms but
does not sufficiently investigate whether interlocking policies made civilian
survival impossible, who controlled those policies, and what happened after
decision-makers received warnings about their consequences.

Specific concerns:

- Item 802 explains the inference standard without explaining mixed purposes.
  A reader could wrongly assume a real military aim excludes genocide.
- Items 818 and 832 record declared objectives and a generic legal defence.
  They test statements more than implementation or consistency with conduct.
- Items 823, 824 and 834 concern improvement or relief. Their significance
  depends on scale, reach, duration, timing and what else continued.
- The hostage-pressure explanation in item 821 requires examination, not
  automatic treatment as an alternative incompatible with genocidal intent.
- Individual incident qualifications can obscure the need to assess the
  cumulative pattern. No one incident need establish the entire allegation.
- Items 798, 820, 826 and 830 should investigate policy mechanisms, recurrence
  and consequences, rather than only emotive episodes or aggregate damage.
- Item 827 appropriately distinguishes provisional measures from judgment, but
  should not imply that public assessment must await a final court judgment.
- Mortality uncertainty should not suggest that inaccessible or destroyed
  records erase corroborated evidence of harm. Israeli estimates deserve
  equivalent scrutiny; actual undercount evidence also matters.
- The Commission of Inquiry's September 2025 intent analysis deserves review.
  The current bank cites its March reproductive-health findings, but does not
  test the later report's integrated reasoning and evidentiary limitations.

This perspective draws particular attention to the relationship between
statements, policy and cumulative conduct. It does not make the Commission's
or an NGO's conclusion a judicial verdict.

Sources for that argument:

- [Amnesty's full December 2024 report](https://www.amnesty.nl/content/uploads/2024/12/Gaza-genocide-report.pdf)
- [Commission of Inquiry, September 2025 legal analysis](https://www.un.org/unispal/document/commission-of-inquiry-report-genocide-in-gaza-a-hrc-60-crp-3/)

### The initial revision plan

The initial plan could overcorrect in the following ways:

- Evidence of military use at one hospital could be generalized to attacks on
  the whole health system. Any addition must retain geographical and temporal
  scope and the continuing protection of civilians and medical care.
- Warnings could be counted without examining safe destinations or means of
  survival. Assess implementation, access and outcomes together.
- Aid entry could be treated as proof against destructive intent without
  considering adequacy, restrictions elsewhere or changes following pressure.
- Comparisons with other urban wars could normalize deprivation or conceal
  incompatible denominators and different access to escape or relief.
- Casualty-ratio debates could consume space while policy-driven deprivation
  and non-lethal prohibited acts receive too little attention.
- A lawful-targeting lesson could become an unproved finding that specific
  attacks were lawful. Teach the rule separately from proving its application.
- A shortage explanation could be accepted because it is conceivable rather
  than because it fits the evidence about decisions and implementation.
- A demand for independent corroboration could quietly become stricter for
  Palestinian testimony than for Israeli military or allied intelligence.
- Adding questions to balance labels could manufacture false equivalence.
  Coverage should follow evidential significance, not equal partisan counts.

## Revised plan after both critiques

### Build a common evidence record first

For every candidate, record:

1. The precise factual proposition, date range, place and relevant population.
2. The evidence, original source and dependence on other sources or datasets.
3. The decision-maker, authority, control and implementation where relevant.
4. The link to a prohibited act, specific intent or attribution.
5. The strongest supported competing explanation, including mixed purposes.
6. Evidence inconsistent with each explanation and what remains unknown.
7. Confidence, limitations and whether those limitations affect the answer.

Use propositions that can actually be checked. Do not require each question
to explain both sides, and do not manufacture four-way opinion questions.
The record supports editorial review; each quiz item tests one discrete fact.

### Proposed allocation: 40 questions

These are coverage targets, not quotas for either conclusion. Final questions
depend on source verification. Each item must work in isolation.

| Coverage | Slots | Required distinctions |
| --- | ---: | --- |
| Legal elements and inference | 10 | Acts and intent; protected group and substantial part; specific intent versus foresight; mixed purposes; reasonable alternatives; proof versus disproof; attribution; other crimes; lawful attack rules; procedural thresholds |
| Historical applications | 4 | Established genocide with military context; partial destruction; failure to prove intent; displacement distinguished from destruction |
| Deaths and evidentiary limits | 4 | Provenance and civilian status; verification and mortality estimation; combatant estimates; observed deaths versus projections |
| Survival conditions and policy | 8 | Food; water; health; reproductive survival; displacement; infrastructure; repeated restrictions; implementation and reversals |
| Military context and attribution | 6 | Armed-group operations; substantiated use of civilian sites; particular targets; official statements; authority and orders; accountability |
| Precautions and humanitarian action | 4 | Warning effectiveness; safe destinations; relief reach and adequacy; changes to operations or access |
| Chronology and competing explanations | 4 | Responses to warnings; recurrence versus correction; deprivation despite different military conditions; documented policy change and outcomes |

The final four slots must concern concrete, sourced episodes or dated
comparisons. They must not ask respondents which overall interpretation is
correct. The integration happens in the reader's opinion, not a scored answer.

### Initial disposition of existing items

- Preserve the substance of 795, 797, 802 and 827, subject to a fresh legal and
  procedural check. Add the missing mixed-purpose distinction.
- Retain four historical applications, provisionally 804, 805, 806 and 809.
  Consider replacing 803, 807 and 808 to free space. Do not introduce a raw
  death-toll comparison with another war.
- Consolidate overlapping mortality-method questions where this remains
  readable. Preserve scrutiny of totals, civilian status and projections;
  make room for scrutiny of competing combatant estimates.
- Rework 817, 818, 831 and 832 around evidence linking statements, authority,
  policies and conduct rather than declarations and generic denials.
- Retain the policy substance of 820 and 821. Investigate necessity, scope,
  effects, alternatives, official explanations and subsequent decisions.
- Treat 798, 819, 829 and 830 as review candidates, not automatic deletions.
  Retain distinct evidence of prohibited acts while reducing duplication.
- Review 823, 824, 826 and 834 together for balanced examination of relief,
  evacuation safety and actual reach. Each item remains self-contained.
- Use 825 to examine documented errors, corrective action and recurrence.
  Do not preassign either exculpatory or incriminating significance.
- Preserve the continuing-obligations principle in 833 alongside substantive
  military evidence. Neither question substitutes for the other.
- Reassess all importance and valence labels after drafting. Do not preserve
  the current labels just to achieve an appearance of balance.

### Chronology and the central intent inquiry

Construct a research timeline of operations, restrictions, official warnings,
humanitarian outcomes, relevant orders, corrective measures and policy
changes. Separate what authorities knew from what they intended; knowledge
and persistence can be evidence of purpose without automatically proving it.

Ask what an explanation accounts for and what it leaves unexplained. Consider
military defeat of Hamas, hostage recovery, displacement, coercion or collective
punishment, group destruction and combinations. These are research hypotheses,
not accepted findings or necessarily mutually exclusive categories.

Do not average intent across all operations or periods. A genocidal episode
is not cancelled by an unrelated lawful operation; one unlawful episode does
not establish the character of the whole campaign. Define the claim's scope
so respondents can answer without guessing which quantifier is intended.

### Equal standards and adversarial review

Apply the same source tests to governments, armed groups, witnesses, NGOs,
international institutions and statistical studies. An official source is
primary evidence of its own statement, not automatic proof of the event.
Organisational independence, access and shared underlying data are separate.
Multiple reports based on one dataset are not independent replications.

For each evidence cluster, write two short objections, one from each
perspective. Resolve legitimate factual objections through sourcing or
qualification; preserve genuine interpretive disagreement. No named body,
including UN Watch, becomes authoritative merely because it criticizes the UN.

### Completion checks and application boundary

- Verify current sources, translations, numerical bases and court status.
- Ensure no question rewards endorsement of the ultimate genocide claim.
- Verify that neither military objectives nor humanitarian measures are
  presented as automatic disproof, and devastation as automatic proof.
- Include the strongest relevant evidence for each material explanation
  without requiring equal numbers of questions or equal evidentiary weight.
- Check at least six to nine foundational and seven supporting items unless
  a documented editorial reason justifies departure from the usual guidance.
- Apply all four-choice, standalone, answer-cue and calibration rules.
- Check glossary entries and use supported tooltip initialisms. The deployed
  editorial API still returned the earlier glossary wording at this review;
  verify deployment and tool availability before any glossary writes.
- Review initial sequencing so neither material counterevidence nor the
  strongest evidence of destructive policy is postponed systematically.
- Produce an item-by-item replacement list, citations, rationales and response
  handling before applying changes. Use substantive resets or retirement
  where required; preserve historical records and audit provenance.
- Keep topic 26 unpublished unless separately instructed to publish it.

## Extension: testing explanations deeply enough

### What success means

A well-informed respondent should be able to distinguish:

- A prohibited act from the additional intent needed for genocide.
- A military objective from a claim that destructive intent was absent.
- Indifference to civilian harm from an intention to destroy the group.
- A hypothetical possibility from a reasonable, evidence-supported account.
- Compatibility with an explanation from evidence favouring that explanation.
- Failure to prove genocidal intent from proof that it was absent.
- The same visible outcome from the same total evidence about decisions.
- A pattern supporting inference from proof supplied by other evidence.
- An overall military purpose from additional purposes or intended means.
- An allegation about a state from an allegation about a particular actor.

These distinctions should be illustrated through sourced law and actual cases,
with most space devoted to the Gaza evidence. Do not turn the bank into a
general logic examination, an abstract legal course or institutional trivia.

### Research the counterfactual as a competing explanation

Define H0 for research as an objective of defeating Hamas, accepting extreme
civilian harm, but without intending physical or biological destruction of
Palestinians as a protected group. This is a hypothesis, not a finding that
such conduct occurred or would be lawful.

Define H1 as relevant prohibited acts committed with group-destructive intent,
including the possibility that military aims also existed. Do not substitute
an unrealistically exclusive extermination objective for the actual allegation.

Keep other supported explanations visible, including coercion, expulsion,
collective punishment, local misconduct and combinations. Do not assume that
each excludes H1 or that failure of H0 proves H1.

For each explanation, identify expectations before choosing the examples.
An account that can accommodate every possible outcome through unverified
auxiliary assumptions is not a useful discriminator. However, no explanation
must predict every detail perfectly to remain reasonable.

Use the actual extent and timing of armed-group resistance. Do not assume
last-man resistance everywhere or classify every civilian site as militarily
used. Conversely, do not assume that civilian damage establishes the absence
of a military objective.

For each selected episode, prepare an editorial comparison containing:

1. What each explanation would lead us to expect, and why.
2. The observed decisions, actions and outcomes, with dates and attribution.
3. Evidence available to decision-makers at the time.
4. Evidence fitting both explanations, which may have limited distinguishing
   power while remaining necessary to establish physical acts or scale.
5. Evidence that fits one explanation better, and the strongest rebuttal.
6. Assumptions required to preserve either explanation and their support.
7. What the record cannot establish and what further evidence would matter.

Do not assign numerical likelihoods without a defensible empirical basis.
Do not ask the quiz-taker to endorse the editor's preferred hypothesis.

### Select connected evidence without dependent questions

Research a small number of policy sequences in depth, rather than accumulating
unrelated dramatic incidents. Candidate sequences include civilian necessities
and access decisions, attacks and destruction of infrastructure, evacuation
and return, and responses to documented errors or warnings. Select only after
checking evidence availability; these are areas to investigate, not findings.

Cover changing military conditions and humanitarian outcomes within those
sequences. Include episodes that challenge each interpretation and identify
correlated evidence, so several reports about one event do not masquerade as
independent confirmation of a policy.

Questions may concern different aspects of a shared research sequence only
when they test distinct facts. Every presented item must repeat sufficient
date, place and policy context to work independently. No references to other
questions, fixed presentation order or combined multi-part answers are allowed.

An explanation should identify the fact's relevance and limits concisely.
It should not bury several untested central propositions in a long essay.

### Workflow and reviewable outputs

1. Fix the opinion's scope and evidence cut-off. Record which interpretation
   of the proposition is being tested and the legal status of cited findings.
2. Build a research inventory of roughly 60 to 70 candidate facts. An inventory
   entry is not yet a question and need not survive review.
3. Complete the competing-explanation records and dated policy sequences.
   Include original documents, corroboration, counterevidence and uncertainties.
4. Draft the strongest opposing accounts using that common record. Identify
   precisely where the disagreement concerns facts, law or interpretation.
   Conduct both perspective reviews without presuming either reviewer is right.
5. Select a 40-item draft using the revised coverage allocation. Give every
   item a reason for inclusion, its inferential role and a reason it cannot
   be replaced by a less consequential fact. The allocation is provisional.
6. Produce four plausible choices, direct sources and concise explanations.
   Apply importance, valence, specialist knowledge, answerability and glossary
   rules. Flag genuinely contested premises instead of forcing a correct key.
7. Audit the draft for missing decisive evidence and misleading inferences.
   Attempt a 40-item version and an expanded version if needed; record exactly
   which essential distinctions the shorter version loses.
8. Present the replacement map and unresolved issues before site changes.
   Applying this plan and publishing the topic remain separate actions.

### Tests that determine whether 40 questions suffice

The draft passes only if a respondent who knows the tested facts can:

- Explain why prohibited conduct and specific intent are both necessary,
  without treating mental elements as punishment for thoughts alone.
- Explain why the mere existence of a non-genocidal scenario does not prove
  that it is a reasonable account of the full evidence.
- Recognize that a reasonable explanation without genocidal intent matters
  to inference, and distinguish that from an unsupported assertion of intent.
- Identify the strongest actual evidence challenging each explanation.
- Separate civilian-harm totals from military context, intent and attribution.
- Assess humanitarian action alongside its scope, outcomes and timing.
- Distinguish an incident, a policy sequence and a campaign-wide allegation.
- Reach either position without losing quiz marks for the position itself.

Run these as qualitative editorial checks, with documented reasons and
examples from the draft. They are not additional scored opinion questions.
If reader or independent subject-expert review is available, use it to test
comprehension and identify ambiguity. Do not describe a desk review as proof
of effectiveness with readers, or a perspective exercise as independent review.

### Capacity assessment and expansion rule

Forty questions is a credible target for a strong introduction to the central
inquiry, including material evidence and limitations. It is a tight budget for
comprehensive treatment of legal interpretation, several years of conduct,
historical applications, mortality methods and competing explanations.

The revised allocation reserves 10 places for legal and inferential tools,
four for history and 26 for Gaza-specific evidence. This requires replacing
overlap, not merely adding new material. It must not compress several distinct
facts into one difficult question to preserve an arbitrary total.

Start with 40. Expand in small groups if the omission audit identifies material
gaps that cannot be repaired by replacing weaker items. A 50 to 60 item bank
is a reasonable contingency, not a promised requirement or evidence threshold.
Do not expand for completeness alone, for equal partisan counts or to achieve
a preferred distribution of opinion responses.

Even a larger bank cannot reconstruct undisclosed intelligence, internal
deliberations or every incident, nor resolve genuinely disputed law by quiz.
Confidence in feasibility is qualitative: good for an informed framework,
more limited for comprehensive evidential coverage, and no guarantee of
consensus or a determinate verdict. Validate the 40-item draft before claiming
that the target has been achieved.

The intended result is a bank that equips readers to assess a cumulative
inference, while distinguishing verified facts from contested attribution and
legal interpretation. It cannot guarantee agreement or eliminate every
criticism; it can make the factual basis and editorial choices inspectable.
